What paid monitoring adds over a free credit freeze

The freeze first; then only the extras a paid plan may add. No guarantees.

Written November 12, 2026. #ad

A credit freeze and a paid identity-monitoring plan are not the same product. The freeze is free and is something you turn on at each credit bureau. A paid plan is a subscription that typically advertises alerts, monitoring of additional data, and some kind of recovery help. This article explains the difference. It does not claim that either step will make you impossible to scam.

What the free freeze is for

As described in our freeze instructions and on the official bureau pages, a freeze restricts access to your credit report at that bureau for the purpose of opening new credit. It does not, by itself:

  • stop charges on a credit card or bank account you already have;
  • block a scam call, a fake delivery text, or a tech-support pop-up;
  • watch the “dark web,” your medical bills, or your tax filings;
  • assign you a company’s recovery agent or insurance policy.

Those limits are a reason to keep reading your existing account statements even after you freeze your credit. They are also the gap that paid plans are marketed to fill — with varying terms you must read on the official site.

What a paid plan may add

Identity-protection subscriptions commonly advertise some mix of: alerts if certain data appears in a monitoring scan, help from a support team if you are already a victim, and, on some tiers, insurance or reimbursement with caps and exclusions. Exact coverage, waiting periods, and exclusions are in each company’s current terms, not in this article. We are not reprinting a feature checklist we have not independently verified line by line on November 12, 2026.

For the dated price comparison, see Aura vs LifeLock: prices published November 12, 2026. Read Aura’s current official pricing before paying; this is an untracked information link while our referral link remains unverified. LifeLock’s official products page is listed in the price article.

A practical order of operations

  1. Place a free freeze at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.
  2. Know how you will lift it when you apply for credit.
  3. Decide whether you also want paid alerts or recovery help. If you do, compare official terms and the dated prices, and ignore any pitch that says a subscription replaces the freeze.
  4. Keep the freeze on even if you subscribe. A plan that “monitors” a credit file is not automatically the same as a freeze that blocks new-account access.

If you already are a victim, start with IdentityTheft.gov regardless of whether you pay for a commercial plan.
